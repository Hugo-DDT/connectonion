# A finished task kept the secret the title had given up

*2026-11-23*

Someone was about to share a screen. Waiting on you was open, and one item on it was marked sensitive. At Cover sensitive the title sat behind the curtain, the way a marked sentence does. They switched to Public only, the setting meant for sharing, and the title left the page. The row did not. Beside the words that the agent had finished was the summary of what it had finished: the same private matter, written out in full, with no mark left on it.

The summary had been derived from the item. The run stored a one-line result, a reason, and a reply, and none of them carried the label the title had. The page already knew how to curtain a passage that still wore `[sensitive]`, and how to take that passage out at Public only. A result drawn later, as plain text next to "finished", never went through that path. Covering the title and then reading the result was like turning a letter over and leaving the postscript face up.

Hiding every finished line would have been simpler, and wrong. An unmarked item's summary is the point of the row, and a personal item is supposed to stay readable at Cover sensitive. The completion label itself is not the secret. What had to inherit the item's level was the derived text: the summary, the reason, the reply, and the report See result opens. A result that brings its own mark is classified by that mark. A result that dropped the mark takes the level of the item it came from. Public only removes those words. Cover sensitive curtains a sensitive result and still shows a personal one. Show all shows them, without printing the mark as a word.

The check is a reader opened on invented items, on a wide window and on a phone. Before the change, Cover sensitive already hid the sensitive title and still painted the summary. After it, that summary, the reason, the reply and the report stay behind the same rule as the title, and the unmarked item's summary stays where it was.
